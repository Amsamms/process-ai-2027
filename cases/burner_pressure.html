Why one heater needs twice the burner pressure

496 days of data, 13 years of mail, one correction from me, and a twin heater as the control case.

The problem

One fired heater has needed about twice the fuel gas pressure at its burners that the burner maker's design curve says, for years. High burner pressure eats the margin to the high pressure trip and limits how hard the heater can be fired. I asked the AI to find out why.

What the AI did

  1. Read 17 monthly plant workbooks (496 operating days), the heater datasheet and the burner design curve.
  2. Searched the work-mail archive and found the same symptom several times over more than ten years, plus an old specialist report.
  3. Measured the gap: in October 2026 the burners ran at 1.89 barg where the curve says 1.02 barg, so they pass only about 68% of the design gas.
  4. When I told it CO shows at the stack at about 6% O2, it reconciled the heater analysers with the shared stack analyser over 17 months and withdrew its first advice to cut excess air.
  5. When I said cleaning only lowered the pressure part of the way, it split the gap into fouling that cleaning removes and a permanent part of about 2x.
  6. Read the 38 vendor files I uploaded and used a twin heater with the same burners as a control case.
  7. Wrote a report where every claim is marked sourced, derived or unknown, and a ranked list of field checks.

The result

The twin heater, with the same burners and the same gas, runs at 0.86 barg, at or above its curve every month, while this heater runs at 1.89 barg. The one plant difference is fuel conditioning: the twin's gas is filtered and arrives at about 125 C, this heater burns raw gas. The leading cause is deposits inside the gas guns and tip bodies, which online cleaning does not reach.

What the engineer still did

I corrected the AI once: my field observation of CO at 6% O2 showed the local analyser over-reads, and the AI withdrew its first finding. The cause is still a ranked hypothesis: a one-gauge pressure survey on every burner and a look inside one gas gun must confirm it.

Time taken

About 1 hour of AI work across 2 days (first draft in 31 minutes; session times)

Guardrails

Plant data and documents were read only; nothing was changed in any plant system. Mail to the unit engineer went only when I asked for it.

Copy it

Put the plant's monthly data workbooks, the equipment datasheets and an index of old mail where the AI can read them. Ask the question in one line, then feed it what you see in the field: the corrections are where the value is.
