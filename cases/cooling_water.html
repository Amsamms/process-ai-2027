Cooling water cut by a third: what must stop?

Circulation fell to 7,500 m3/h. The AI read the design files, found it was not just a pump, and timed the tanks.

The problem

The cooling water unit that serves a group of process units could only circulate about 7,500 m3/h, roughly a third short of normal demand. I uploaded its design files and asked what the scenarios and options were: which units would have to stop, and how long the plant could live like that.

What the AI did

  1. Read the uploaded design files: the cooling water unit's operating manual and design basis, the tank farm design basis and the manuals of the connected utilities.
  2. Worked out the gap: about a third of normal demand, with four pumps each rated a little above 7,500 m3/h, two running and two on standby.
  3. Showed that 7,500 m3/h is less than one healthy pump should deliver, so the cause is a choked network, a valve, the driver or the suction, not just a pump that is off, and gave a simple pressure test to tell which.
  4. Checked the tank farm: there is no light naphtha tank, so stopping the isomerization unit also stops the splitter and the hydrotreater upstream; the residue tanks hold about two weeks.
  5. On my question about the sour naphtha tank, found that its only pumps feed the hydrotreater, which would be down, so the tank has no outlet and fills in 1 to 3 days.
  6. Wrote a plain Word document with two shutdown scenarios, both water balances, both tank limits and an action list.

The result

Scenario 1 (three units down) restores a margin, but the sour naphtha tank forces a crude rate cut within 1 to 3 days. Scenario 2 (the second crude unit down too) gives about twice that margin and pushes the tank limits later. The document asked for circulation to be restored and listed the pressure and valve checks to find the cause.

What the engineer still did

The tank that actually governs was my catch: I asked about the sour naphtha tank late in the job, and that moved the limit from about two weeks to 1 to 3 days. I also set the scenarios and the scope. All hold-up times are design numbers from empty tanks, and the cause of the restriction still needs the pump curves and field readings.

Time taken

About 50 minutes from my question to the final document, by my typed requests and the file times.

Guardrails

It worked only from the files I uploaded and did not contact anyone. The document went to me as a file; I decided where it went next.

Copy it

Upload the design basis and operating manual of the limited unit plus the tank farm basis, and state the limit in plain numbers. Ask for scenarios, a water (or steam, or power) balance for each, and the time each tank takes to fill.
