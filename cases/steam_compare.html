Case 08Planning4 November 2026

Which unit makes steam more cheaply?

The AI first said no. I explained the real goal three times; it re-ran the balance and drew one chart.

The problem

Another unit was down and the refinery was short of steam. Unit A, a hydrogen plant, was kept at its minimum load mainly for its export steam, while much of its hydrogen had no other use. The idea: shut Unit A, run the boilers harder and load up Unit B, another unit that we believed makes steam more efficiently. Does that hold?

What the AI did

  1. Read that day's daily averages for both units, the steam and fuel sheets, and a second day (10 October) at higher loads for comparison.
  2. Checked both design manuals: minimum loads, export steam at design and at turndown, alarms and trips.
  3. Gave a first verdict of no: shutting Unit A loses 21 to 26 t/h of steam, and Unit B's steam barely rises with load. It also noticed that Unit B ran with more excess air than design at low load.
  4. Took three corrections from me and re-ran the steam balance each time, saying plainly when it had missed the point.
  5. Drew a three-panel chart, then, when I said it was too busy for management, one simple chart with the design case added.

The result

On my goal (the same steam at lower cost), the swap works on paper: the boilers' spare capacity adds about 17 t/h, and Unit B at 85 to 100% load covers the remaining 4 to 9 t/h, for about 7,000 Nm3/h less natural gas and a use for the spare hydrogen. The chart shows Unit B makes about 1.7 times the steam of Unit A per unit of natural gas at design load, and about 2.3 times at today's loads.

What the engineer still did

The first verdict was wrong for my question, because it solved for more steam. I had to explain three things only an operator knows: the spare hydrogen has no other use, the heaters' burners cannot take hydrogen, and the boilers have proved their spare capacity in a field test. The boiler gas figure is the AI's estimate, and Unit B's real steam response still has to be confirmed by raising it in steps before Unit A is shut down.

Time taken

44 minutes from question to the management chart, by the transcript times, including my three corrections.

Guardrails

Read-only plant data and manuals. The charts were mailed only to my own work inbox, when I asked; nothing went to anyone else.

Copy it

Send the AI the day's plant data export and give it the design manuals. Ask it to assess the idea, then correct it with what you know from the field and ask it to re-run the numbers each time. Ask for a one-chart version for management.
