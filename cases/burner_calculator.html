A burner pressure calculator in 47 minutes

Vendor curves read off old scans, checked against the vendor's own data, built into a live spreadsheet.

The problem

When a burner pressure looks high, the first question is: what should it be, for today's gas and today's firing? The vendor curves are drawn for one reference gas, some only exist as old scans, and one group of heaters has a single design point. I asked for a spreadsheet that answers the question for six heaters.

What the AI did

  1. Searched the technical database and the work-mail archive and found the burner datasheets, the curves and a 2023 team thread with its workbooks.
  2. Traced one heater's colour curve chart and calibrated the log axes of another heater's black and white scan on its gridlines.
  3. Tested the method before building: from one vendor curve it predicted the vendor's other curves within 0.03 barg.
  4. Built an 11-sheet workbook where every result is a live formula, with a real day of gas analysis and flows as the example.
  5. Recalculated it, found its own bug (a constant named like a cell address read as 0), fixed it, and matched an independent Python model within 0.0003 barg.
  6. Wrote down what is weak: one heater group has only a single design point, about 10% uncertain.

The result

A live spreadsheet: paste the fuel gas analysis, enter burners in service, gas temperature and flow, and it gives the design burner pressure. It also works backwards: at a measured pressure it gives the flow the burners should pass, so a shortfall points to plugged tips, a throttled valve or a meter error. On the licensor's own 2025 example it gives 1.27 barg against their 1.23.

What the engineer still did

Two inputs are still placeholders (gas temperature on two heater groups), and the AI flagged that the 1 September lab report contradicts itself (its composition and its density give different molecular weights). Those need the plant's answers, not the AI's guess.

Time taken

47 minutes from question to a checked workbook (session times)

Guardrails

Read only on documents and mail; no plant system was touched. The workbook was sent to a colleague only when I asked for it.

Copy it

Keep the vendor datasheets and curves, even old scans, where the AI can read them. Ask for a live-formula workbook, then ask it to prove the method on the vendor's own curves before it builds.
