Reviewing a colleague's air cooler report

A first review in 12 minutes, one point that went too far, and the question that put it right.

The problem

A colleague sent our department an assessment of five air coolers cleaned from the outside in June. Three reached their design outlet temperature and two did not. For one gas cooler, the report asked the rotating equipment team to check the fan. Before it went up to my manager, I asked the AI to review it.

What the AI did

  1. Read the report from my mail, with the trend chart of each cooler, and confirmed that cold approach (outlet minus air temperature) is the right measure for an air cooler.
  2. Checked each claimed gain against its chart: one cooler's gain was 20 C in the text and about 16 C on the chart.
  3. Asked for a frequency or a trigger in place of "regular cleaning".
  4. Mailed the review to me and the author 12 minutes after my request, then an HTML version with recommendations and a corrected draft.
  5. On the gas cooler, first argued that the fan was fine because the temperature drop equalled design. After my question, it withdrew that and showed the effectiveness had fallen from 0.81 to 0.64.
  6. Listed what would separate the two possible causes: the gas flow against design, the air temperature at the time, and an outlet transmitter.

The result

The gas cooler dropped the gas by 76 C, the same as design, but against a much bigger driving force: its effectiveness was about 0.64 against 0.81 by design. A healthy cooler would have dropped it by about 95 C, to about 51 C instead of 70. So the fan check in the report was justified.

What the engineer still did

I caught the AI's mistake. A hotter inlet should give a bigger drop, so an equal drop hides a weaker cooler. The author's revised report kept the fan check, and I sent it to my manager as written. Whether the cause is the fan or more gas than design is still open; it needs field data.

Time taken

12 minutes from my request to the first review mail; the corrected view came the same morning (mail and note times).

Guardrails

The AI read the report from my mailbox and wrote only to me and the author. The judgement on the report, and what went to my manager, stayed with me.

Copy it

Forward the report to a mailbox the AI can read and ask for a review against the charts. Then ask it the questions you would ask a junior engineer, and watch whether it changes its mind for a good reason.
