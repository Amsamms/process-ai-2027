Why the caustic wash tower kept upsetting

1,800 hours of plant data, the lab archive and old mail: two suspected causes tested, one left standing.

The problem

A caustic wash tower that polishes fuel gas had been running unsteadily since September: its level swung and the operators had cut the caustic circulation on purpose. I wanted to know why. My own suspicion was a low fresh caustic make-up; some colleagues blamed the low water make-up.

What the AI did

  1. Read the unit workbook (daily averages since January 2024) and the lab archive of the spent caustic.
  2. Searched the work mail and found a 2023 study that had linked this kind of blockage to weak caustic.
  3. Balanced the sulfur load and showed the caustic used had roughly doubled since 2025, while the make-up was cut to about 0.25 m3/h.
  4. Checked my hourly upload (1,800 hours, no gaps) against the daily averages, then found that every upset had the same signature.
  5. Tested both suspects against the upset hours: the water make-up was ruled out, the caustic make-up was the only variable that separated the calm periods from the upset ones.
  6. Wrote the report with charts and, on my request, mailed it to a process colleague from my mailbox; later revisions added manual references and a minimum wetting rate check against the tower and packing datasheets and a standard handbook.

The result

From 1 September the make-up sat at about 0.25 m3/h against about 0.34 to 0.40 needed. The caustic had no free strength left on 16 of 39 samples, and high pressure drop hours grew from 5 to 39 per week. After a fresh caustic charge on 8 to 10 October the tower ran four days at full circulation with zero upset hours.

What the engineer still did

I uploaded the hourly data, stated the competing ideas and asked for the test. The root cause and the report are a desk study: the make-up rate to use is the unit team's decision, and the daily check kept following the tower afterwards.

Time taken

About 37 minutes from the first question to the root cause answer (session times), including my hourly data upload in between.

Guardrails

It read the plant data and mail archive only; it never touched the plant. The mail went out only because I asked for it, from my own mailbox.

Copy it

Put the unit workbook, the lab archive and an hourly export in one folder, give the AI a searchable copy of your work mail, then ask the question in your own words. Ask it to test each suspected cause against the upset hours, not just to describe the trends.
