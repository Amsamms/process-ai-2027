Case 16Everyday tool2 June 2026

Ask the library: what does more C7+ do?

One plain question. Four helpers searched the manuals and books: five effects, each with its source page.

The problem

A process question that comes up often: what happens if more C7+ (heptane and heavier) gets into the feed of the isomerization unit? The answer is spread over several licensor manuals, the unit's own manuals and textbooks, and nobody has time to read them all for one question.

What the AI did

  1. Sent four helpers in parallel over my technical library: the licensor's isomerization manual, the unit's own manuals before and after the revamp, the splitter and reformer manuals, and the textbooks.
  2. Compared what they found: all four agreed on five bad effects.
  3. Listed them in the manuals' own words: yield lost to light gas, a higher reactor temperature rise, lower product octane, faster coking of the catalyst and more heavies to reject downstream.
  4. Pointed to the usual root cause: a cut-point upset in the naphtha splitter upstream.
  5. On request, found the exact page for each effect and cut those pages out of the manuals as 5 PDFs, checking that each cut page holds the quote.
  6. Mailed the pages to my work mail, then sent a richer, easier-to-read version when I asked.

The result

Five effects, each backed by the manual's own page. The key numbers: the manual prefers C7+ held to 2 or 3% of the feed, lists 5% as a typical limit, and the splitter is designed for less than 3 wt% C7+ in its overhead with over 95 mol% of the C6 recovered.

What the engineer still did

I asked for the source pages, not a summary, and for a mail I could read comfortably. The answer is what the manuals say in general; applying it to our feed still needs our own lab results and splitter data.

Time taken

The answer was on screen within 15 minutes (my next request came at minute 15); the 5 source pages were cut out by minute 25, by the file times.

Guardrails

Read-only access to the library. The mail went only to my own work mail, and only when I asked for it.

Copy it

Put your manuals and textbooks in one folder the AI can read, and ask the question in plain words with 'search everything'. Ask for the source page of every claim, cut from the original document, not retyped.
