How little amine can we live with?

A neighbouring unit had to take one train out. Three independent methods gave the same floor: about 20% less.

The problem

A neighbouring unit had to shut down one of its two amine regeneration trains and asked us how little lean amine our unit could run on. Too little amine and the sour gas leaves untreated; too much and the remaining train is overloaded. They needed a number they could plan around.

What the AI did

  1. Read the operating manual's amine chemistry pages, the tray and pump data sheets, and two years of daily plant data and lab results.
  2. Sent two helpers in parallel: one to the neighbouring unit's manuals (the supply side), one to every hydraulic minimum (trays, pumps, valves, meters).
  3. Compared the lean amine meter with the rich amine meter day by day and found one meter reading about 70 m3/h low from late January to late April 2026, so those days were excluded.
  4. Worked out the floor three independent ways: the manual's own formula, the measured rich amine loading, and the days the plant had already run low and stayed on spec.
  5. Found an old study in the work mail archive and settled a disagreement between two tray sheets by opening the original 1999 page.
  6. Wrote a report in which every number is marked as sourced (document and page) or derived (calculation shown).

The result

Our unit can go down from about 144 to about 115 m3/h of lean amine, about 20% less. The three methods agree at 83 to 90 m3/h for the main absorber before margin. A surprise for the neighbouring unit: their design allows us only 71.6 t/h of rich amine with one train down, which assumes 50 wt% amine; at today's 35 wt% that cannot be reached unless the H2S load roughly halves.

What the engineer still did

I told it that it took too long for a small question. It agreed: the answer was ready after about 15 minutes, and the extra checks only confirmed it, so it saved the lesson 'answer first, then deepen'. The step-down itself is for the unit team, in small steps with lab checks; the answer is a planning number, not a field result.

Time taken

About 50 minutes from question to answer, by the transcript times; the report file was written at minute 41.

Guardrails

Read-only access to the manuals and plant data. The summary mail went out only after I asked for it, to the colleague I named, with me in copy.

Copy it

Put the design manuals and the daily plant data where the AI can read them, and ask the question in plain words with 'verify from two sources'. Ask for every number to be marked sourced or derived, and ask for the short answer first.
